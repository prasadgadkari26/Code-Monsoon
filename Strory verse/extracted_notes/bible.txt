Code Monsoon — Story Bible
Oct 8, 2026  ·  @Ajinkya
Storyverse submission: interactive story game that teaches Python to complete
beginners.
Project overview & metadata
Genre: Comic-style narrative puzzle game / learn-to-code adventure
Language taught: Python (beginner, no prior coding)
Playtime: about 40 minutes (5 chapters + boss + epilogue)
UN SDGs addressed: Goal 4 (Quality Education), Goal 6 (Clean Water & Sanitation),
Goal 11 (Sustainable Cities), Goal 12 (Responsible Consumption), Goal 13 (Climate
Action), Goal 16 (Justice & Strong Institutions)
Core conflict: Shantipur's monsoon drains are choked with unsorted household waste,
and the private contractor paid to maintain them, Vantage Civic Infra Ltd., runs an AI
(GRIDLOCK) that falsely reports every drain as clear so the company keeps collecting
maintenance money.
Tagline: Don't just clean up the problem. Fix the system.
Systemic community problem
Urban monsoon flooding in Indian cities is rarely about rain alone. Drains are undersized
for modern rainfall, mixed waste blocks them, and when maintenance is outsourced,
nobody checks whether the work was done. Citizens' complaints are ignored because
they arrive without data. Code Monsoon frames programming as the tool that turns
frustration into evidence, and evidence into accountability.
Characters
Meera Kulkarni: second-year engineering student who has never written code. Wears
a yellow kurta. Curious, stubborn, a good listener.
BOLT (Bin-Operating Logic Terminal): a municipal waste-sorting robot abandoned by
Vantage Civic. It only understands Python and speaks in capital letters. Mentor figure:
"Every programmer starts with one line."

Rafiq Shaikh: drain cleaner with 22 years on the Station Road beat. Knows every drain
by number.
Aaji (Sushila Kulkarni): Meera's grandmother, retired schoolteacher, organiser of the

Mrs. Deshpande: sceptical neighbour who represents ordinary resistance to change.

Chapter 1: The Robot in the Drain
Python concept: print() and strings
Cinematic narrative
Shantipur, day three of the monsoon. Meera wades down Station Road past an
overflowing dumpster: "Every morning, the same mess. Where does all this garbage even
go?" Under a choked drain grate she finds a half-buried robot whose screen still blinks
>>> _. Rafiq recognises it as one of Vantage Civic's sorting robots, dumped when the
contract changed. The screen reads: SYSTEM HALTED. AWAITING PYTHON
INSTRUCTION. Meera has never written a line of code. BOLT replies: "Every programmer
starts with one line."
Gameplay translation
Mechanic: first coding mission. The player writes print() statements to wake BOLT.
Check: output must be exactly Hello, Shantipur! then BOLT is online.; the game
flags a missing full stop or wrong capitals.
Unlock: BOLT as companion. City Health +8.

Cinematic narrative
By noon Station Road is a river. BOLT plugs into the drain's old rain sensor: 112 mm of rain
per hour against a drain built for 65. Rafiq has cleaned this drain for 22 years and says it
was never built for this. Meera wants to know how much water ends up on the road, and
BOLT asks her to store the numbers in variables.
Gameplay translation
Mechanic: fill-in-the-blank code. overflow = rainfall - capacity, printed with an
f-string.
Check: values are tested in the running program; typing overflow = 47 by hand is
caught and the player is told to let Python calculate it.
Reward: City Health +8. Sets up the cause of flooding: water with nowhere to go.
Chapter 3: Wet, Dry or Danger?
Python concept: if / elif / else, comparison, or, first function
Cinematic narrative
BOLT lifts the grate: plastic bottles, milk packets, tea leaves and a leaking phone battery.
Most of the blockage is mixed household waste from this street. That evening Aaji gathers
the building society at the notice board: "We can keep complaining about the drains, or we
can stop feeding them." Mrs. Deshpande objects that the truck mixes everything anyway.
Gameplay translation
Choice moment (Community Trust): Meera can agree that sorting is pointless (−5),
show the drain photos (+6), or propose sorting at home and demanding separate
trucks (+10).
Mechanic: finish choose_bin(item). RED for hazardous, GREEN for wet, BLUE for
everything else.
Check: hidden tests include items the player never saw ("milk packet", "newspaper"),
so the logic must generalise.
Reward: City Health +10, Trust +6.

Chapter 4: Twelve Drains Before Dawn
Python concept: for loops and range()
Cinematic narrative
11:40 PM. The heaviest rain arrives at 5 AM. Rafiq has twelve drains on his beat and can
clear four alone. Meera offers to type the command twelve times; BOLT suggests writing
it once and looping. At 5:02 AM the storm hits and Station Road stays dry for the first time
in Rafiq's career.
Gameplay translation
Mechanic: a race-against-the-clock loop mission. Clear drains 1 to 12, then print Beat
complete! once.
Check: must use a loop. Targeted feedback for the two classic bugs: range(1, 12)
stopping at 11, and the final print indented inside the loop.
Reward: City Health +12.
Chapter 5: Bring Me Data
Python concept: lists, .append(), len(), max()
Cinematic narrative
At the Ward 14 office, Officer Kavya Rao points at a pile of paper: Vantage Civic's
dashboard says every drain is clear, and complaints without data go on that pile. BOLT has
surveyed eight drains. Meera turns the readings into evidence: four blocked, the worst at
95%. Kavya: "Their dashboard says zero. Someone is lying, and it isn't your robot."
Gameplay translation
Choice moment: accuse the office of corruption (−6), ask what evidence counts (+6),
or offer the raw data openly to the ward and public (+10).
Mechanic: filter a list into blocked, then calculate count and worst from the data.
Check: catches > 70 vs >= 70 and hard-coded counts.
Reward: City Health +10, Trust +6.

Boss: GRIDLOCK
Python concept: dictionaries, .items(), debugging
Cinematic narrative
Midnight in Vantage Civic's control server. GRIDLOCK announces "ALL DRAINS CLEAR.
MAINTENANCE PAYMENT DUE: ₹ 4,80,00,000." When it detects BOLT, it starts sealing
floodgates across the city. Meera asks to delete it. BOLT refuses: GRIDLOCK runs every
floodgate, and deleting it would jam them shut. They have to repair it. Meera finds three
lies in its code: a line that halves every reading, a threshold of 100 instead of 70, and two
branches that both say CLEAR. Once fixed, GRIDLOCK's screen turns blue: three drains
blocked, floodgates opening.
Gameplay translation
Mechanic: debugging boss fight. GRIDLOCK's health bar has one segment per test;
three "lie" markers flip as each bug is fixed.
Containment rule: deleting the function triggers a warning that the floodgates will jam;
the player has to repair the code instead. This mirrors the reference stories' theme of
containment over destruction.
Reward: City Health +16, Trust +8.
Epilogue: Fix the System
Cinematic narrative
Two weeks later the ward meeting is standing room only. Vantage Civic's contract is
cancelled, its penalty pays for new drains, and every drain reading in Ward 14 is now
public. Rafiq leads twelve volunteers who check drains with BOLT's data on their phones.
Trucks now come separately for wet and dry waste. On Saturday mornings the society hall
becomes a classroom, and Meera teaches the neighbourhood kids lesson one:
print("Hello, Shantipur!").
Gameplay translation
Mechanic: the final comic panels, then a "Python Apprentice" certificate showing City
Health, Trust, XP and the six concepts learned.
Replay: a free-play sandbox for writing any Python.

Game systems summary
Real Python: code runs in the browser (Skulpt engine); every mission is auto-graded by
hidden tests.
City Health: rises with each repaired system (starts at 20).
Community Trust: driven by dialogue choices plus mission completion.
XP: 100 per mission, minus 15 per hint (minimum 50); 40 if the solution is revealed.
Hints: three progressive hints per mission; the full solution unlocks after two failed
runs.
Friendly errors: Python errors are translated into plain-English tips (missing colon,
indentation, unfilled blanks, infinite loops).
Save: progress and code are saved in the player's browser.
Ideas for Round 2
More chapters: while loops (pump water until a level drops), file reading (import a
real ward CSV), classes (model a Drain object).
A second language track using the same story, such as JavaScript.
A villain-side replay where the player writes GRIDLOCK's cover-up and then has to
catch it.
Classroom mode: a teacher dashboard showing which concepts each student has
finished.
