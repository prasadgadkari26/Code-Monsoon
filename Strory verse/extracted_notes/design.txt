1 · Project overview & metadata
TITLE Code  Monsoon
GENRE Comic-style  narrative  puzzle  game  /  learn-to-code  adventure
LANGUAGE  TAUGHT Python,  for  complete  beginners  with  no  prior  coding
PLAYTIME About  40  minutes:  5  chapters,  a  boss  chapter  and  an  epilogue
PLATFORM Web  browser  (desktop  and  mobile).  Real  Python  runs  inside  the  page;  nothing  to
install.
SETTING Shantipur,  a  fictional  Indian  city  during  the  monsoon.  Ward  14,  Shantipur  East.
UN  SDGS 4  Quality  Education 6  Clean  Water  &  Sanitation 11  Sustainable  Cities
12  Responsible  Consumption 13  Climate  Action 16  Justice  &  Strong  Institutions
CORE  CONFLICT
Shantipur's  monsoon  drains  are  choked  with  unsorted  household  waste,  and  the  private  contractor  paid
to  maintain  them,  Vantage  Civic  Infra  Ltd.,  runs  an  AI  called  GRIDLOCK  that  falsely  reports  every  drain  as
clear  so  the  company  keeps  collecting  maintenance  money.
SYSTEMIC  COMMUNITY  PROBLEM
Urban  monsoon  flooding  in  Indian  cities  is  rarely  about  rain  alone.  Drains  are  undersized  for  modern
rainfall,  mixed  waste  blocks  them,  and  when  maintenance  is  outsourced  nobody  checks  whether  the
work  was  done.  Citizens'  complaints  are  ignored  because  they  arrive  without  data.  Code  Monsoon
frames  programming  as  the  tool  that  turns  frustration  into  evidence,  and  evidence  into  accountability.
WHY  A  STORY  TEACHES  CODE  WELL
Every  Python  concept  is  introduced  at  the  exact  moment  the  story  needs  it:  print() to  wake  a  robot,
variables  to  measure  rain,  if/ else to  sort  waste,  loops  to  clear  twelve  drains  before  dawn,  lists  to
build  evidence,  and  debugging  to  expose  a  lie.  The  player  always  knows  why they  are  learning  each  idea.

SECTION  3
The game dashboard
The  ward  map  is  the  game's  home  screen.  Each  card  is  one  chapter  and  one  part  of  the  city  to  fix.  The  bar  at  the
top  tracks  the  player's  progress  at  all  times.
Ward  map  dashboard. Chapters  unlock  in  order;  finished  ones  show  FIXED,  and  the  boss  chapter  is  marked  in  red.  The  top  bar  shows
City  Health,  Community  Trust,  XP  and  whether  the  Python  engine  is  ready.
DASHBOARD
ELEMENT
WHAT  IT  SHOWS
City  Health Starts  at  20  (a  flooded  city)  and  rises  with  every  repaired  system.  Ends  around  84.
Community  Trust Goes  up  or  down  with  Meera's  dialogue  choices,  and  up  with  each  completed  mission.
XP 100  per  mission,  minus  15  for  each  hint  used  (never  below  50);  40  if  the  full  solution  is

Python  ready Shows  that  the  in-browser  Python  engine  has  loaded.

How a chapter plays
Every  chapter  follows  the  same  five  steps,  shown  as  a  progress  strip  at  the  top  of  the  screen:  Story  →  Choice
→  Manual  →  Mission  →  Debrief.  The  Choice  step  appears  in  Chapters  3  and  5.
Step  1,  Story. Comic  panels  are  revealed  one  at  a  time  with  captions  and  speech  bubbles.  BOLT  speaks  from  a  green  terminal.

--- PAGE 6 ---
Step  3,  BOLT's  Manual. A  one-page  lesson  on  the  chapter's  Python  idea,  with  a  short  example  program  and  its  exact  output.
Step  4,  Mission. The  player  edits  real  Python  and  presses  Run.  BOLT's  checks  turn  green  one  by  one;  when  all  pass,  the  Debrief  unlocks.

SECTION  4  ·  CHAPTER  1
The Robot in the Drain print() & strings
SDG  11
Cinematic narrative
Shantipur,  day  three  of  the  monsoon.  Meera  wades  down  Station  Road  past  an  overflowing  dumpster.  Under  a
choked  drain  grate  she  finds  a  half-buried  robot  whose  screen  still  blinks  >>> _.  Rafiq  recognises  it  as  one  of
Vantage  Civic's  sorting  robots,  dumped  when  the  contract  changed.  The  screen  reads:  SYSTEM  HALTED.
AWAITING  PYTHON  INSTRUCTION.
Meera:  "Python?  I've  never  written  a  single  line  of  code."
BOLT:  "EVERY  PROGRAMMER  STARTS  WITH  ONE  LINE."
Gameplay translation
Mechanic: first  coding  mission.  The  player  writes  print() statements  to  wake  BOLT.
Checks: output  must  be  exactly  Hello, Shantipur! then  BOLT is online.
Smart  feedback: a  missing  full  stop  or  wrong  capitals  gets  a  specific  tip.
Reward: BOLT  joins  as  companion.  City  Health  +8,  Trust  +4.
print("Hello, Shantipur!")
print("BOLT is online.")
CHAPTER  2
Reading the Rain variables & numbers
SDG  13 SDG  6
Cinematic narrative
By  noon  Station  Road  is  a  river.  BOLT  plugs  into  the  drain's  old  rain  sensor:  112  mm  of  rain  per  hour  against  a
drain  built  for  65.  Rafiq  has  cleaned  this  drain  for  22  years  and  says  it  was  never  built  for  this.  Meera  wants  to
know  how  much  water  ends  up  on  the  road,  and  BOLT  asks  her  to  store  the  numbers  in  variables.
Gameplay translation
Mechanic: fill-in-the-blank  code;  the  result  is  printed  with  an  f-string.
Checks: variable  values  are  tested  inside  the  running  program.  Typing  overflow = 47 by  hand  is  caught,
and  the  player  is  told  to  let  Python  calculate  it.
Reward: City  Health  +8,  Trust  +4.  Establishes  the  cause  of  flooding:  water  with  nowhere  to  go.

CHAPTER  4
Twelve Drains Before Dawn for loops & range()
SDG  11 SDG  6
Cinematic narrative
11:40  PM.  The  heaviest  rain  arrives  at  5  AM.  Rafiq  has  twelve  drains  on  his  beat  and  can  clear  four  alone.  Meera
offers  to  type  the  command  twelve  times;  BOLT  suggests  writing  it  once  and  looping.  At  5:02  AM  the  storm
hits,  and  Station  Road  stays  dry  for  the  first  time  in  Rafiq's  career.
Gameplay translation
Mechanic: clear  drains  1  to  12  with  one  loop,  then  print  Beat complete! once.
Checks: the  code  must  use  a  loop.  The  game  spots  the  two  classic  bugs:  range(1, 12) stopping  at  11,  and
the  final  print  indented  inside  the  loop.
Reward: City  Health  +12,  Trust  +5.
Mission  feedback. Here  the  player  used  range(1, 12) .  BOLT's  checks  show  what  failed,  the  yellow  note  explains  why,  and  the  first
hint  is  revealed.

CHAPTER  5
Bring Me Data lists
SDG  16 SDG  11
Cinematic narrative
At  the  Ward  14  office,  Officer  Kavya  Rao  points  to  a  pile  of  paper:  Vantage  Civic's  dashboard  says  every  drain  is
clear,  and  complaints  without  data  go  on  that  pile.  BOLT  has  surveyed  eight  drains.  Meera  turns  the  readings
into  evidence:  four  blocked,  the  worst  at  95%.
Kavya:  "Four  blocked  drains.  Their  dashboard  says  zero.  Someone  is  lying,  and  it  isn't  your
robot."
CHOICE  MOMENT  ·  COMMUNITY  TRUST
Accuse  the  office  of  corruption  −6
Ask  what  evidence  counts  +6
Share  the  raw  data  openly  with  the  ward
and  public  +10
MISSION
Filter  a  list  into  blocked,  then  calculate
count with  len() and  worst with  max().
The  game  catches  > 70 vs  >= 70 and  hard-
coded  counts.  Reward:  Health  +10,  Trust  +6.
levels = [35, 82, 91, 40, 77, 12, 95, 60]
blocked = []
for level in levels:
    if level >= 70:
        blocked.append(level)
count = len(blocked)
worst = max(levels)
print(f"{count} of {len(levels)} drains blocked. Worst: {worst}%")
# Output: 4 of 8 drains blocked. Worst: 95%

Gameplay translation: a debugging boss fight
Three  hidden  lies in  GRIDLOCK's  code:  a  line  that  halves  every  reading,  a  threshold  of  100  instead  of  70,  and
two  branches  that  both  say  CLEAR.
Boss  health  bar: one  segment  per  hidden  test.  Each  "lie"  marker  flips  to  a  tick  when  that  bug  is  fixed.
Containment  rule: deleting  the  function  triggers  a  warning  that  the  floodgates  will  jam.  The  player  has  to
repair  the  code  instead.
Reward: City  Health  +16,  Trust  +8.
Mid-fight. Two  lies  are  fixed  (HALVING  ✓ ,  THRESHOLD  ✓ ),  but  both  branches  still  say  CLEAR,  so  every  check  fails  and  the  health  bar
stays  full.

Victory. GRIDLOCK  turns  blue  and  reports  the  truth:  three  drains  blocked,  floodgates  opening.  "Contained,  not  destroyed."
# The repaired function
def drain_report(levels):
    report = {}
    for name, level in levels.items():
        if level >= 70:
            report[name] = "BLOCKED"
        else:
            report[name] = "CLEAR"
    return report

SECTION  7
Game systems
SYSTEM HOW  IT  WORKS
Real  Python Code  runs  in  the  browser  with  the  Skulpt  engine.  Each  mission  is  graded  by  hidden  tests  that  inspect
both  printed  output  and  variable  values.
City  Health Rises  with  every  repaired  system,  from  20  at  the  start  to  about  84  at  the  end.
Community
Trust
Driven  by  two  dialogue  choices  (Chapters  3  and  5)  plus  mission  completion.
XP 100  per  mission,  minus  15  per  hint  (minimum  50);  40  if  the  full  solution  is  shown.
Hints Three  progressive  hints  per  mission.  The  full  solution  unlocks  after  two  failed  runs  or  all  hints.
Friendly  errors Python  errors  are  translated  into  plain-English  tips:  missing  colon,  wrong  indentation,  unfilled  ___
blanks,  and  infinite  loops  (stopped  after  4  seconds).
Targeted
feedback
Common  beginner  mistakes  get  a  specific  explanation,  such  as  range()  off-by-one,  >  vs  >= ,  or
=  vs  == .
Save Progress  and  the  player's  code  are  saved  in  their  browser.
